## What it is

FAST models the intensities of a skull-stripped brain as a mixture of tissue classes, each a Gaussian (three by default: CSF, grey matter and white matter, in the order of their intensity on a T1w), and finds the class of every voxel by expectation-maximisation with a hidden Markov random field that favours neighbours of the same class. It starts from a k-means of the brain's intensities (or from given intensities, `-s`, or from FSL's tissue priors registered onto the image, `-a`), estimates a smooth multiplicative bias field along with the classes and divides it out at each iteration, and ends with the partial volume of each tissue in each voxel: the fraction of CSF, grey and white matter a voxel at a boundary holds, from a second random field over the mixtures of neighbouring classes. Its outputs: the segmentation (`_seg`), the partial-volume maps (`_pve_0` to `_pve_2`) and their segmentation (`_pveseg`), the mixel types, and on request each class's posterior probability (`-p`), its binary image (`-g`), the bias field (`-b`) and the corrected image (`-B`); with `-S n` it segments several registered images together.

fMRIPrep calls it once per subject, on the T1w its brain extraction made (denoised, N4-corrected and skull-stripped), in its anatomical workflow (`anat_fit_wf/fast`): `fast -I 0 -N -p -g -S 1 <T1w brain>`, without a bias field (N4 corrected it before). Its partial-volume maps become the CSF, grey- and white-matter probability maps fMRIPrep writes (`label-CSF_probseg` and the others), from which its CompCor masks are made.

```bash
larmorx fsl fast -I 0 -N -p -g -S 1 T1w_brain.nii.gz             # fMRIPrep's call
larmorx fsl fast -b -B -o out T1w_brain.nii.gz                   # the bias field and the corrected image
larmorx fsl fast -S 2 -B -o out T2w_brain.nii.gz T1w_brain.nii.gz   # two channels (channel 1 named last)
```

```python
import larmorx as lx

r = lx.fsl.fast("T1w_brain.nii.gz", bias_iterations=0, no_bias=True, probabilities=True,
                segments=True)
csf, gm, wm = r.partial_volumes   # lx.Image each, on the input's grid
labels = r.segmentation.data      # 1 CSF, 2 grey matter, 3 white matter, 0 outside the brain
```

FSL is distributed under the FSL Licence, which allows non-commercial use only, so LARMORX keeps its FSL tools in two implementations: the exact replica translated from FSL's source, in the separate package `larmorx-nc` (`pip install larmorx-nc`), and a clean-room original in LARMORX itself, written from a behavioural specification without FSL's code. `implementation="auto"` (and `larmorx fsl fast`) runs the replica when `larmorx-nc` is installed and the clean-room original otherwise; `implementation="original"` (on the command line `LARMORX_IMPLEMENTATION=original`) chooses the original. The clean-room original is written from FAST's behavioural specification ([specs/fast.md](repo:specs/fast.md)), the paper (Zhang, Brady and Smith, IEEE TMI 2001) and black-box runs of FSL's program, never from FSL's code: every option, message, exit status and failure case, one channel or any number of them (`-S 9` and more too, which the replica refuses), the tissue priors registered by FLIRT's clean-room original in the same process, `exp` and `log` correctly rounded on every platform. It is built for speed: the random field's sweeps keep FAST's in-place order and run as a pipeline of planes that gives the one-thread result bit for bit at any thread count, FSL's blocked sums run block by block in parallel in their order, the partial volumes' 3 + 3 × 101 likelihoods per voxel go through a batched `exp` with the scalar function's bits (below, *Speed*). The replica follows FSL 6.0.7.23, runs none of FSL's programs and needs no FSL installation (with `-a` it registers the priors with FLIRT's replica, in the same process); its FSL libraries (newimage, the C library's random numbers and maths as FSL's build calls them, Armadillo 14.2.1's matrix arithmetic on OpenBLAS's LAPACK for several channels) are those every FSL replica of `larmorx-nc` shares with mcflirt, BET and FLIRT. How to read what follows: [the validation guide](/validation/#reading).

## The verdicts at a glance {#verdicts}

The clean-room original is written without FSL's code, so the question for it is whether it gives FSL's result within the thresholds of [specs/fast.md](repo:specs/fast.md) §11 (FSL's exit status, files and messages, numbers to 4 significant digits; per-class Dice at least 0.999 and 99.9 % of the brain's voxels identical; partial volumes within 0.001 on average and 0.02 at the 99.9th percentile; the bias field within 1e-4), and how close it comes: each case it passes is classified against FSL as built and against the same unmodified FSL with CORE-MATH's correctly rounded `exp`, `log`, `expf` and `logf` preloaded, whose difference from FSL as built is FSL's own last-bit band.

{{ ev.agreement('fsl-fast', 'original') }}

The original's record runs 87 cases against FSL 6.0.7.23 ([the record](repo:docs/validation/fsl-fast.md)): 60 within the thresholds, 25 rejected by both alike (the same message and exit status), 1 refused where FSL reads past the smaller of two channels (FSL-104), and 1 outside. Of the 60, 13 give FSL's values bit for bit, 36 give bit for bit what FSL computes with correctly rounded maths (*identical with correctly rounded maths*: fMRIPrep's three calls and every single-channel case that differs from FSL as built at all; the differences come from glibc's last bits alone), 10 lie within FSL's own band (several channels: the original equals FSL with correctly rounded maths but in a few partial volumes, each one fraction step of 0.01 away: near ties, most likely decided by the last bits of the matrix arithmetic, which the original computes with its own LU, as only the cases with several channels show them), and one, `multi/s2-options`, has 18 such partial-volume values where FSL's band has 15 (*open*). The one outside the thresholds, `multi/s5`, has five channels of which the first is nearly a combination of the last two: each class's 5 × 5 covariance is nearly singular, and its result is decided by the matrix arithmetic's last bits (the original against itself with one rounding changed moves further than it lands from FSL; following LAPACK's operation order is the replica's job). Random testing's trial for the original (25 minutes, 9,005 random calls on the replica's realistic draws, FSL's runs through the oracle cache): 8,823 agree; of the 182 that differed, 153 came from the suite's own image reader (FSL's zstd outputs, a NIfTI-2 quaternion), 13 from a NaN bias field outside the brain mask and 12 from messages, all corrected in the original or the suite and kept as four permanent cases of the record; 3 are explained (two noise-free channels whose ill-conditioned covariances move with the matrix arithmetic's last bits; a rank-deficient covariance where the original's LU finds an exactly zero pivot and aborts as FAST does on a singular matrix, while FSL's rounding leaves a tiny pivot and it finishes), and one message stays open (a degenerate class whose mean FSL prints as `-nan` and the original as 1, the images agreeing; how FSL comes to it is answered by the specification since 2026-11-03, for the original's next part).

The replica is a translation of FSL's source, so the question for it is whether it gives FSL's bytes: every file FAST writes (each image's header and data), its standard output and error, and its exit status.

The record runs 87 cases against FSL 6.0.7.23 ([the record](repo:docs/validation/fsl-fast-replica.md)): 59 give FSL's files byte for byte, in 27 FSL refuses the input or stops and the replica does the same, with the same message and exit status (*rejected by both*; since 2026-11-03 one of them is an image whose first voxel is NaN, where FAST stops with exit status 255 and the replica's default segments it, FSL-203), and in one FSL reads and writes past the smaller of two channels of different sizes, which the replica refuses (*expected divergence*: FSL-104). They cover fMRIPrep's call on three oracle runs (an adult, the data panel's 3.5-year-old and its glioma patient; fMRIPrep's own partial-volume maps the same too), every option with its short and long name (the image types, two to four classes and one, the bias field's iterations and smoothing, the random field's weights, `--nopve`, the outputs, `-s`), FSL's tissue priors (`-a`, `-A`, `-P`, registered by FLIRT's replica), one to eight channels (`-S 2` to `-S 8`: Armadillo's and OpenBLAS's arithmetic followed to 8 × 8 covariances), 2D and 4D images, integer images, NaN, negative and very large values, and every message, exit status and failure case of the command line. Each case also runs the replica's default mode, where LARMORX corrects FSL's deterministic defects and names them (below); since 2026-11-03 that includes the single-channel variance (FSL-202), so the default's outputs of every single-channel case differ from FSL's, by the amounts the examples show. Random testing's trial (half an hour, 17,497 random calls on brain-extracted phantoms and crops of a real T1w, every option, one to four channels, FSL's priors with a drawn matrix): 16,768 agree, 725 are rejected by both alike, and 4 differed, all where FLIRT cannot write the registered priors and FAST then uses priors it never allocated (FSL-101): the replica's `reproduce` mode now stops, or runs on, as FAST does, and the three kinds are cases of the record.

## The bias field {#bias}

FAST's default, which fMRIPrep's call turns off: the bias field estimated along with the classes, in the logarithm of the intensities (where a multiplicative field adds), from each voxel's residual against the class means weighted by its posteriors, smoothed by a Gaussian (its extent `-l`, 20 mm by default), and removed at each iteration (the record's case `opt/bias-outputs`, on the same T1w at half resolution). Choose the bias field or the corrected image above the panels. ## FSL's tissue priors (-a), and a correction by default {#priors}

With `-a` and a standard-to-input matrix, FAST brings FSL's tissue priors (avg152T1's CSF, grey- and white-matter maps) onto the image with FLIRT and starts the segmentation from them (the record's case `priors/a-verbose`, on MNI152NLin2009cAsym's T1w). The priors are stored as 8-bit integers, and FLIRT writes its resampled values in that type truncated toward zero; the replica's default rounds them (FSL-09), so it starts from slightly other priors and its result differs from FSL's, which its reproduce mode gives byte for byte (since 2026-11-03 the default also corrects the variance, FSL-202, as on every single-channel call). The difference *replica, default − reproduce mode* shows what the correction changes. The clean-room original registers the priors with FLIRT's clean-room original in the same process: its reproduce mode gives FSL's result with correctly rounded maths, its default mode makes the same correction.

## Several channels {#multi}

`-S 3` segments three registered images together, here MNI152NLin2009cAsym's T1w, T2w and BOLD reference (the record's case `multi/s3`): each class a Gaussian in three dimensions with its own 3 × 3 covariance, a bias field per channel. The last image named, the T1w, is channel 1: it names the outputs and orders the classes. The clean-room original computes the covariances' determinants and inverses with its own LU decomposition, not LAPACK's: it equals FSL with correctly rounded maths but in a few partial volumes one fraction step away, within FSL's own band.

## Speed {#speed}

FSL's `fast` is single-threaded. On fMRIPrep's call the clean-room original takes 2.4 to 5.2 s on one thread, 9 to 11 times faster than FSL, and 0.4 to 0.9 s on 14 threads, 47 to 67 times; the replica, a translation, is 1.4 to 1.9 times faster than FSL's build ([the report](repo:docs/benchmarks/fsl-fast.md): informal, one run each, on a machine shared with another job; FSL's time is its run stored by the oracle cache, not run again). The original's results are the same bits at any thread count; its peak memory on fMRIPrep's call, as the memory test measures it at one thread, is 152 MiB against FSL's 707.

## Corrections by default {#corrections}

The replica corrects FSL's deterministic defects by default and warns, with `upstream_defects="reproduce"` (`LARMORX_UPSTREAM_DEFECTS=reproduce`) giving FSL's behaviour byte for byte where FSL's is defined ([register](repo:docs/findings/upstream-defects.md)). `-p`'s probability maps outside the brain mask (and everywhere with `--nopve` and priors, or without an initialisation iteration) are memory FAST never writes: the replica writes 0 there in both modes and names it (FSL-96); fMRIPrep's call meets it (FSL's maps held zeros there in its three runs of the record; in two multi-channel cases one file held other values, which the record sets to 0 before comparing). With several channels and `-N`, the bias field `-b` saves and `-B` divides by is never computed: no field in both modes (FSL-97). With several channels, FAST's sort of the classes gives a class another class's covariance and posteriors when the classes move in a cycle (given intensities in a cyclic order, for example): each class keeps its own by default (FSL-98). A smoothness that is not a number, a bias smoothing of 0 or less and fewer than one class are refused by default, with the reason (FSL-99; FAST uses them as given or aborts). FAST aborts when `FSLDIR` is unset, even without priors: the replica segments (FSL-100). When FLIRT cannot register the priors, FAST says they are not used and then crashes on priors it never allocated: the replica segments without them (FSL-101). `-a` runs FLIRT through the shell with the names unquoted: the replica passes each name whole (FSL-102). The brain mask leaves out the voxels of the image's maximum when it is 2^24 or more: corrected (FSL-103). Channels of different sizes, which FAST reads past their ends, are refused in both modes (FSL-104). Since 2026-11-03, with the specification's answers to the clean-room original's questions: with one channel FAST takes each class's variance in the logarithm of the intensities as the mean of the squares minus the square of a single-precision mean, which cancels most of its digits; the replica's default squares the mean in double precision (FSL-202: it changes the default outputs of fMRIPrep's call, toward the exactly computed statistics, as the examples show); an image whose first voxel is NaN gives FAST NaN intensity extrema and an empty brain mask, and FAST stops with exit status 255: the replica's default takes the extrema over the values that are numbers and segments it (FSL-203). Inside `-a`, FLIRT's replica corrects its own defects too (FSL-09, above). The clean-room original makes the same corrections by default with the same notices (FSL-202 and FSL-203 are to follow in its next part), and its reproduce mode follows FSL where FSL's behaviour is defined (for a NaN `-H`, FAST's estimate of the smoothness; FSL-98's pairing; FSL-101's runs and aborts; FSL-102's split of the names; FSL-104 refused in both modes); inside `-a`, FLIRT's clean-room original corrects FSL-09 as the replica does.

## Known limits {#limits}

Both implementations are experimental. Each record's standard tier holds fMRIPrep's calls on three oracle runs; the full tiers (fMRIPrep's call on every oracle run), the data panel's other images and long random testing run in the final validation phase. The clean-room original is held to thresholds, not to FSL's bytes: with several channels its own matrix arithmetic leaves a few partial volumes one fraction step from FSL's, and nearly singular covariances (five channels, two of them nearly a combination of the others) are decided by the arithmetic's last bits; the single-channel variance in the log domain cancels most digits, which makes FSL's own band wide on small classes: measured since (2026-11-03), it is a defect of FAST, FSL-202, corrected by default in the replica, and the clean-room original's default follows in its next part (until then it is held to FSL's band). More than eight channels (`-S 9` and more): the clean-room original segments them; the replica refuses them in both modes with a message, as FSL's results there follow OpenBLAS's kernels for larger matrices, which it does not reproduce. With several channels whose covariances turn NaN (an empty class), a NaN can carry other payload bits than FSL's (the values and their NaN-ness are FSL's). Two behaviours of the multi-channel segmentation are kept as FAST's until they are measured on real T1w and T2w pairs: its initialisation, which pairs each channel's classes in the order of that channel's own intensities (channels of opposite contrast can start from empty classes, and FAST then ends with every voxel in one class), and its bias field. The other five platforms are held to LARMORX's own Linux output by the [golden tests](repo:docs/validation/golden.md) (the clean-room original: fMRIPrep's call, the bias field, two channels, the priors, the defects' switches and a 2D image). The benchmark is informal: one run each, on a shared machine.
