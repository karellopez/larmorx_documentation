## What it is

FLIRT finds the affine matrix that best aligns an input image to a reference: a cost function compares the two images under a candidate matrix (the correlation ratio by default; mutual information, normalised correlation, least squares and others on request), a search over rotations on a coarse grid finds the basin, and Powell's method refines it over a pyramid of blurred and subsampled copies, from 8 mm to the images' own resolution, by the steps of a schedule. The boundary-based cost (`-cost bbr`) instead samples the input's intensity on both sides of a white-matter surface taken from a segmentation of the reference (`-wmseg`), and rewards a contrast across it: the registration fMRIPrep relies on to put each BOLD run on its T1w. `-applyxfm` applies a given matrix, `convert_xfm` inverts, concatenates and decomposes matrices.

fMRIPrep calls it once per BOLD run, in its boundary-based registration (`bold_reg_wf/fsl_bbr_wf/flt_bbr`), after mri_coreg's initial alignment: `flirt -in <boldref> -ref <T1w brain> -wmseg <white matter> -init <initial matrix> -schedule $FSLDIR/etc/flirtsch/bbr.sch -dof 6 -searchcost bbr -basescale 1`.

```bash
larmorx fsl flirt -in boldref.nii.gz -ref T1w_brain.nii.gz -dof 6 -cost bbr \
    -wmseg wm.nii.gz -init init.mat -schedule bbr.sch -omat bold2t1.mat -out bold_in_t1
larmorx fsl convert_xfm -omat t12bold.mat -inverse bold2t1.mat
larmorx fsl avscale --allparams bold2t1.mat boldref.nii.gz       # rotations, scales, skews
larmorx fsl rmsdiff bold2t1.mat other.mat boldref.nii.gz         # the RMS deviation in mm
```

```python
import larmorx as lx

r = lx.fsl.flirt("boldref.nii.gz", "T1w_brain.nii.gz", init="init.mat", cost="bbr",
                 search_cost="bbr", dof=6, base_scale=1, schedule="bbr.sch",
                 wm_seg="wm_mask.nii.gz")
r.matrix          # the 4 × 4 matrix, as -omat writes it
r.image           # the BOLD reference on the T1w's grid
```

FSL is distributed under the FSL Licence, which allows non-commercial use only, so LARMORX keeps its FSL tools in two implementations: the exact replica translated from FSL's source, in the separate package `larmorx-nc` (`pip install larmorx-nc`), and a clean-room original in LARMORX itself, written from a behavioural specification ([specs/flirt.md](repo:specs/flirt.md)), the papers (Jenkinson and Smith 2001; Jenkinson et al. 2002; Greve and Fischl 2009) and runs of FSL's programs as black boxes, without FSL's code. `implementation="auto" | "replica" | "original"` chooses (on the command line `LARMORX_IMPLEMENTATION`); `auto` takes the replica when `larmorx-nc` is installed, else the original. The original follows FSL 6.0.7.23 too; it has every program, option, message and exit status of the replica, and computes FLIRT's costs, Powell's optimiser and its matrix algebra on the engine LARMORX's head-motion correction uses ([lx.mri.hmc](/tools/mri-hmc/)), so the two share one implementation of the correlation ratio and Woods' cost. It is built for speed: the boundary points, the correlation ratio's bins and the search's grid run in parallel and are summed in a fixed order, so the result is the same at any thread count; its memory peak stays within 10 % of FSL's (plus the interpreter's 8 MiB) on every measured call. The replica follows FSL 6.0.7.23, runs none of FSL's programs and needs no FSL installation; its FSL libraries (newimage, its cost functions, the matrix arithmetic of Armadillo 14.2.1 on OpenBLAS's LAPACK) are those every FSL replica of `larmorx-nc` shares with mcflirt and BET. How to read what follows: [the validation guide](/validation/#reading).

## The verdicts at a glance {#verdicts}

A clean-room original is written without FSL's source, so its record holds it to thresholds (specs/flirt.md §13): exit statuses and messages FSL's (the numbers in them read as numbers), every file FSL writes identical, a registration's matrix within a displacement of FSL's over the reference's brain (its 95th percentile within 0.1 mm for the boundary-based and 6-dof registrations, 0.5 mm for the others, or within FSL's own variability where the registration is poorly conditioned), and its image then within a correlation of FSL's. A case is *bit-identical* when every value it compares is FSL's; *within the original's own variability* when the matrix is beyond the fixed threshold but within FSL's own on the same call with its input perturbed by noise of half an intensity unit (or, for the worked examples, within that variability measured for them); *open* when a file only meets its threshold, or misses it.

The original's record runs the replica's standard cases and 13 of its own (the verbose levels and `-debug`) against FSL 6.0.7.23 ([the record](repo:docs/validation/fsl-flirt.md)): 206 of its 214 compared cases are within the thresholds, 85 are rejected by both alike, and 6 are expected divergences where FLIRT crashes or never finishes. Bit-identical: `-applyxfm` with nearest neighbour and trilinear interpolation, `convert_xfm`, FLIRT's other programs' printed results and files (`avscale`, the coordinate programs, `rmsdiff`, `makerot`, `midtrans`, `pointflirt`, `applyxfm4D` but for its sinc and spline kernels' last bits, `pairreg`'s messages, `extracttxt`, `aff2rigid`), the schedules' and the command line's messages. Within the thresholds: every verbose level's lines (FSL's lines, their numbers those of the original's own path; at levels 5 and above each run of cost evaluations counted as one, their number following the optimiser's path) and `-debug`'s volumes before the registration (FSL's headers, the values within 10⁻⁶ of their range). A registration that is not bit-identical by design ends a little elsewhere: its costs follow FSL's arithmetic within their last bits, and the optimiser's path then parts. fMRIPrep's three boundary-based calls are within 0.011 mm of FSL's matrices at the 95th percentile of the brain (two of them within FSL's own variability, the third 0.0101 mm against FSL's 0.0086 mm), the default 12-dof registration within 0.033 mm. The 8 cases it misses are ill-posed registrations (`-cost normmi` and `leastsq`, weighted `normcorr`, `normmi` and the `woods` warning's place in the stream) and FSL-91 and FSL-92's reproduced runs, where every cost is constant and FLIRT's matrix is an artefact of its search; the record's findings explain each ([findings](repo:docs/findings/fsl-flirt-original.md)). Random testing's trial of every program (9,854 realistic random calls, 24 minutes) found 2,712 differences; the kinds that were the original's mistakes were fixed, each checked on the trial's cases of its kind (the headers of `-applyisoxfm` and of a reference without coordinates, the zstd and bzip2 outputs of integer images, the forms of an unset sform or qform, the messages of BBR without a boundary point, the `-dof 3` messages, the coordinate programs' and avscale's last digits, `convert_xfm -inverse`), one is a defect of FSL's own (FSL-111, below), and the registrations whose paths part on random, often ill-posed pairs stay open, with the sinc and spline kernels' last bits.

The replica is a translation of FSL's source, so the question for it is whether it gives FSL's bytes: every file FSL writes (the matrix as text, the registered image's header and data), its exit status and its messages.

The record runs 305 cases against FSL 6.0.7.23 ([the record](repo:docs/validation/fsl-flirt-replica.md)): 211 give FSL's files byte for byte, in 87 FSL refuses the input and the replica refuses it alike, with the same message and exit status (*rejected by both*), and in 7 FSL crashes or never finishes, where the replica goes on, or stops with the reason, as its register entry says (*expected divergences*: FSL-71, FSL-72, FSL-75, FSL-93, FSL-204). Each case also runs the replica's default mode, where LARMORX corrects FSL's deterministic defects and names them; on fMRIPrep's calls it changes nothing. Random testing's first trial (3,945 random calls on small synthetic images) found two kinds of difference, both fixed and kept as cases of the record; the rest were settled next ([findings](repo:docs/findings/fsl-flirt.md)): FSL corrupting its heap on small images, traced to its background value (FSL-75, below), a base-scale quotient FLIRT takes in single precision (fixed), and two defects LARMORX's default had corrected without naming them (named now). Two shorter trials of the new paths (12,376 and 3,274 random calls: quaternions and the other programs) found one missing message, the background value's order of NaN voxels and makerot's notices, all fixed. A half-hour trial of part 3's paths (209,657 random calls: midtrans, pointflirt, applyxfm4D, the schedule's `alignpaxes` and parameter subsets, extracttxt) found applyxfm4D's double-precision arithmetic, the order of NaN voxels in its background value, a singular matrix in a cost evaluation and a volume too large to allocate (FLIRT's exceptions), and extracttxt's messages for a range before the first line: all fixed and kept as cases; the sign of NaN results from images with NaN or infinite voxels in `reproduce` mode is left open ([findings](repo:docs/findings/fsl-flirt.md)). ## FLIRT's other programs {#programs}

The programs FSL builds with FLIRT, each with FSL's command line in `larmorx fsl <program>` and a Python function: `avscale` decomposes a matrix into rotations, translations, scales and skews (and the half transforms); `img2imgcoord`, `std2imgcoord` and `img2stdcoord` map coordinates between images through a matrix; `rmsdiff` gives the RMS deviation between two matrices over a sphere of 80 mm, or the largest and RMS displacement over a mask; `makerot` writes a rotation about an axis through a centre. What they print is FSL's text, character for character, in the record's `programs/` cases below and in the probe calls behind them (avscale on 400 random matrices), from both implementations.

```python
import numpy as np

m = np.loadtxt("bold2t1.mat")
d = lx.fsl.avscale(m, "boldref.nii.gz", all_params=True)       # avscale --allparams
xyz = lx.fsl.img2imgcoord([[10, 20, 30]], "boldref.nii.gz", "T1w.nii.gz", xfm=m)
lx.fsl.rmsdiff(m, np.eye(4), "boldref.nii.gz")                   # mm
r = lx.fsl.makerot(30, axis=(1, 2, 3), centre=(4, 5, 6))         # 30 degrees
```

Since part 3, the rest of the programs FSL builds with FLIRT: `midtrans` finds the transform to the "middle" of several transforms of images to one template (a Procrustes average about the template's centre); `pointflirt` the affine matrix that maps one set of points onto another in the least-squares sense; `applyxfm4D` resamples every volume of a series through one matrix or one per volume, in the series' own data type (char, short, int, float or double, with double precision where FSL keeps it); and FLIRT's scripts: `pairreg` registers two brains refined with their skulls, `extracttxt` prints the lines around a match in a text file, `aff2rigid` approximates a standard-space matrix by a rigid one that keeps the anterior commissure, the AC-PC line and the mid-sagittal plane. Their matrices and images are FSL's bit for bit in the replica's record (`midtrans`' and `pointflirt`'s singular-value decompositions follow FSL's own LAPACK word for word). The clean-room original prints the same text and writes the same files: its fits' matrices within 10⁻⁹ of FSL's (a unit of the printed results' last digit), `applyxfm4D`'s nearest-neighbour and trilinear images bit for bit, its sinc and spline images within their last bits, and `pairreg`'s matrix within the record's displacement threshold.

## Speed {#speed}

Wall time of the whole command (reading, registering, writing), one run each, on fMRIPrep's call on the record's three oracle runs and on the record's real registrations ([benchmark](repo:docs/benchmarks/fsl-flirt.md); informal: the machine was shared with another agent's runs, and the original was timed at its part 2's third step, before the specification's answers changed its arithmetic). FSL and the replica run on one thread; the original on one and on 14, with the same result at any thread count. Its boundary-based cost computes the boundary points' terms in parallel: fMRIPrep's call is 1.2 to 1.3 times FSL's speed on one thread and 12 to 14 times on 14, the field map's search 3 times on one thread (it skips the steps that cannot reach the mask). The small correlation-ratio registrations are line searches of a few tens of thousands of points, one evaluation after the other: on one thread at FSL's speed (0.9 to 1 times), 3 to 4 times on 14; SIMD sampling is the next step.

## Corrections by default {#corrections}

Both implementations correct FSL's deterministic defects by default and warn, in the same way unless said otherwise below; with `upstream_defects="reproduce"` (`LARMORX_UPSTREAM_DEFECTS=reproduce`) the replica gives FSL's behaviour byte for byte, the original within its record's thresholds ([register](repo:docs/findings/upstream-defects.md)). FLIRT ends with exit status 0 after any error past its command line (an image or matrix it cannot read, a matrix of the wrong size, an unknown `-bbrtype`): LARMORX exits 1 with FLIRT's message, and 0 in `reproduce` mode (FSL-68). What FSL leaves undefined (a schedule's matrix name it cannot interpret, `setoption bbrstep` below 1, a matrix file with a word that is not a number, `-pedir` without a field map) LARMORX refuses in both modes, with the reason (FSL-70 to FSL-73). FSL's background value of a volume 3 voxels wide (a level of FLIRT's pyramid on a small image, or the padding of such an input) comes from a buffer too small for the border it writes: FSL corrupts its heap there, and then aborts, crashes or finishes; LARMORX takes the value over the whole border by default, the values that fit FSL's buffer with `reproduce` (FSL's result where it finishes), and names FSL-75 in both modes; a single-voxel volume, which has no border, is refused. FLIRT shares with mcflirt the integer outputs' clamping (FSL-09), the treatment of infinite voxels (FSL-14) and the order of NaN voxels in a background value (FSL-22). `makerot` divides its axis by the square root of its length, so a rotation about an axis that is not of unit length turns by the angle times that root: corrected by default (FSL-76); an axis or centre of other than three values, which FSL reads past, is refused (FSL-77). `midtrans` exits with status 0 after an error of its computation (a singular matrix): exit 1 by default (FSL-78). `applyxfm4D`'s spline interpolation of an integer image keeps the spline's coefficients and values in the image's integer type, a coarser and shifted spline: computed as for a float image by default (FSL-79); with `-3D` and sinc it uses a blackman kernel of width 7 instead of the rectangular kernel of width 9 it asks for: the rectangular kernel by default (FSL-80); both are named only where the written image changes. FSL 6.0.7.23's `aff2rigid` stops with a Python error under NumPy 2 before writing its matrix: the matrix is computed by default (FSL-81), FSL's error with `reproduce`. The background value's buffer (FSL-75) is corrected in `applyxfm4D` and mcflirt's replica too. The boundary-based cost's normals are scaled by the reference level's voxel sizes, not the segmentation's (FSL-67): measured on fMRIPrep's three calls and an anisotropic segmentation, computing them in millimetres moves the results by 0.02 mm or less and brings no gain, so they stay FLIRT's in both modes. With `-anglerep quaternion` FLIRT composes the search's starting points, which hold Euler angles, as quaternions, so most of them lose their rotation: composed as Euler angles by default, FLIRT's composition with `reproduce`, named in both modes (FSL-90). A `-refweight` or `-inweight` with no voxel of at least 0.01, and an input or reference of a single voxel, make every cost constant and FLIRT's matrix an artefact of its search: refused by default, FLIRT's run with `reproduce` (FSL-91, FSL-92). A voxel size of 0 in a header makes the base scale 0 and FLIRT never finishes: counted as 1, as reading the image counts it, by default and refused with `reproduce`; `-basescale 0` refused in both modes (FSL-93). `applyxfm4D` reads an image stored as unsigned bytes as signed ones, so its values 128 to 255 enter the interpolation, the background value and the spline's coefficients as negative numbers (a profile 100, 140 interpolated 0.4 of the way gives 13 instead of 116), while `flirt -applyxfm` reads the same image as stored: the clean-room original reads it as stored by default and as FSL with `reproduce`, named where a written value changes (FSL-111, found by its black-box runs); the replica the same since 2026-11-03 (its default names FSL-111 where the bytes written differ; its reproduce mode reads as FSL, without a notice). `pointflirt` with two coordinate files that hold no point crashes FSL after its messages: the replica refuses it in both modes after FSL's lines (FSL-204, since 2026-11-03), the clean-room original is to follow.

## Known limits {#limits}

Both implementations are experimental. The records' standard tier holds fMRIPrep's calls on three oracle runs; its full tier (fMRIPrep's 22 calls on every oracle run with BOLD runs) and the data panel's other images run in the final validation phase. Every program and script of FSL's FLIRT package is replicated except the coordinate programs' `-warp` (FNIRT's warp fields, refused with a message until FSL's warp-field libraries are ported) and the scripts that need FSL's other programs (`standard_space_roi`, `flirt_average`, `epi_reg`: with `fslmaths`, FAST and the field maps). With NaN or infinite input voxels in `reproduce` mode, a NaN result can carry the other sign than FSL's (`nan` against `-nan`): an all-NaN matrix from a schedule that optimises relative to a row, and NaN voxels of `applyxfm4D`'s spline interpolation; the values are NaN in both. The clean-room original misses its record's thresholds in 8 cases (above); beyond them, what is open: the sinc and spline kernels' last float bits, the registrations' paths on random, often ill-posed pairs, `-debug`'s volumes' headers on oblique and anisotropic random images, the sign of one of avscale's residuals (the specification answers each since 2026-11-03, [specs/flirt.md](repo:specs/flirt.md) §14, for the original's next part: the sinc's last bits, for one, are the C library's single-precision sine, which its reproduce mode would need); its random testing was one trial of 24 minutes, the long trials run in the final validation phase. The other five platforms are held to LARMORX's own Linux output by the [golden tests](repo:docs/validation/golden.md): both implementations' programs have their cases.
