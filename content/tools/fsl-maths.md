## What it is

`fslmaths <input> [operations] <output>` reads an image, applies each operation in turn to the current image and writes the result: arithmetic with a number or another image (`-add`, `-sub`, `-mul`, `-div`, `-rem`, `-mas`, `-max`, `-min`), thresholds by value or by percentage of the robust range (`-thr`, `-thrp`, `-thrP` and their upper forms), voxelwise functions (`-exp`, `-log`, the trigonometric functions, `-sqrt`, `-abs`, `-bin`, `-nan`, `-index`, `-grid`, seeded noise), kernels and the operations that use them (dilations and erosions, mean and median filters, Gaussian smoothing, convolutions by direct sums or by the FFT, subsampling, edges, hole filling), mean normalisation, reductions along a dimension (`-Tmean`, `-Tstd`, `-Tmax`, `-Tmedian`, `-Tperc`, `-Tar1` and their X, Y and Z forms), p-values, ranks and z ↔ p conversions, regions, band-pass temporal filtering (`-bptf`), the threshold-free cluster enhancement (`-tfce`, `-tfceS`), ROC curves (`-roc`) and the diffusion tensor's decomposition. `-dt` chooses the type it computes in (float by default, or double), `-odt` the output's.

fMRIPrep calls it in niworkflows' BOLD reference workflows (`combine_masks`: `fslmaths <mask> -mul <mask> <out>`, the reference's brain mask as the product of two masks) and, with `--project-goodvoxels`, fourteen times in its goodvoxels mask, with four fslstats calls between them.

```bash
larmorx fsl fslmaths mask_a.nii.gz -mul mask_b.nii.gz combined.nii.gz
larmorx fsl fslmaths bold.nii.gz -Tstd -div mean.nii.gz cov.nii.gz
```

```python
import larmorx as lx

combined = lx.fsl.fslmaths("mask_a.nii.gz", "-mul", "mask_b.nii.gz")   # the command line's operations
smooth = lx.fsl.smooth("mean.nii.gz", sigma=3.0)                       # one typed wrapper per family
```

FSL is distributed under the FSL Licence, which allows non-commercial use only, so LARMORX keeps its FSL tools in two implementations: the exact replica translated from FSL's source, in the separate package `larmorx-nc` (`pip install larmorx-nc`), and a clean-room original in LARMORX itself, written from a behavioural specification without FSL's code. `implementation="auto"` (and `larmorx fsl fslmaths`) runs the replica when `larmorx-nc` is installed and the clean-room original otherwise; `implementation="original"` (on the command line `LARMORX_IMPLEMENTATION=original`) chooses the original. The clean-room original is written from fslmaths' behavioural specification ([specs/fslmaths.md](repo:specs/fslmaths.md)), FSL's usage text and documentation and black-box runs of FSL 6.0.7.23, never from FSL's code: every operation of the usage text and the hidden ones, every data type in and out, every message, exit status and failure case, the defects with their switches, `exp`, `log`, the trigonometric functions and `pow` correctly rounded on every platform. It is built for speed: the separable filters and the direct sums run a row of voxels at a time, `-bptf` in blocks of voxels, the reductions per column, every parallel loop summing in a fixed order, so its result is the same bits at any thread count; it holds one copy of the image (the input's values moved into the computation, the output's to the writer); where FSL convolves a large kernel by its FFT the original takes the direct sum too, faster than FSL's FFT on every kernel measured (below, *Speed*), in the orientation of FSL's direct sum (FSL-192). Where the specification leaves a convention open it follows FSL's as black-box runs measured it: the Gaussian's exponent `i² (d d)` per axis, the geometry of a new grid, `-roc`'s candidates, TFCE's checks in FSL's order. The replica follows FSL 6.0.7.23 and needs no FSL installation; what fslmaths needs from FSL's libraries (newimage's image functions, kernels, morphology, convolutions, connected components and TFCE, the Cephes probability functions of fsl-cprob, Armadillo's Kiss FFT and symmetric eigen-decomposition) is translated once into `larmorx-nc`'s shared core, beside what mcflirt, BET, FLIRT, FAST and topup use; BET's own `fslmaths` and `fslstats` calls run the same code. How to read what follows: [the validation guide](/validation/#reading).

## The verdicts at a glance {#verdicts}

The clean-room original is written without FSL's code, so the question for it is whether it gives FSL's result within the thresholds of [specs/fslmaths.md](repo:specs/fslmaths.md) §21 (FSL's exit status, messages, file names and headers; the values bit for bit, the double-precision functions within one unit in the last place per function, the sums within 10<sup>−5</sup> of FSL's largest value with 99 % of the voxels within 4 units in the last place, TFCE, `-tensor_decomp` and `-roc` by their own measures), and how close it comes: each case's verdict is read from what the record measures.

{{ ev.agreement('fsl-maths', 'original') }}

The original's record runs 442 cases against FSL 6.0.7.23 ([the record](repo:docs/validation/fsl-maths.md)): the replica suite's cases, 8 of its own (`orig/`) and 17 permanent cases of random testing. 358 give FSL's values in every file, with FSL's headers, messages and exit status (fMRIPrep's four `combine_masks` calls and the goodvoxels mask's fourteen among them, the `-s 5` at 3 × 3 × 3.3 mm too); 63 are rejected by both alike; 13 are within the thresholds without being bit for bit, each with its reason in the case explorer: FSL's FFT path on two kernels, `-subsamp2` and `-subsamp2offc` under the floating types (FSL's arithmetic there is a question for the specification), the double-precision `-ztop` and `-ranknorm` (the last bits of Φ and Φ<sup>−1</sup>), `-tensor_decomp`'s eigenvectors, the same up to their sign (FSL's signs are LAPACK's, the original's each one's largest component positive), and one `-roc` area 10<sup>−7</sup> from FSL's; and 8 are expected divergences: FSL's FFT path on 5 kernels and `-subsamp2offc`'s means on 2, within the sums' bound but not its share of voxels within 4 units in the last place, and `-dt double -tan` (GLIBC-07, [below](#glibc)).

The replica is a translation of FSL's source, so the question for it is whether it gives FSL's bytes: every file fslmaths writes (each image's header and data), its standard output and error, and its exit status.

The record runs 421 cases against FSL 6.0.7.23 ([the record](repo:docs/validation/fsl-maths-replica.md)): 358 give FSL's files byte for byte, in 61 FSL refuses the input or stops and the replica does the same, with the same message and exit status (*rejected by both*), and 2 differ by one unit in the last place of a few values, where FSL's result comes from glibc 2.35's mathematical functions that are not correctly rounded and the replica's is (GLIBC-07, *expected divergence*, [below](#glibc)). They cover fMRIPrep's calls (the four `combine_masks` calls of three oracle runs and the goodvoxels mask's fourteen), every operation and option of the command line in every data type, the odd headers and sizes, FSL's own failure cases, and the defects LARMORX corrects by default: each case also runs the replica's default mode, which names every correction where it changes the result (below).

## One call per operation family {#families}

fslmaths' operation families on the same real series, one call each: arithmetic with images (the temporal SNR), a threshold by the robust range, a voxelwise function, reductions along time, morphology, the three kinds of filters (Gaussian smoothing by separable sums, a mean filter whose large spherical kernel fslmaths convolves by the FFT, a median filter), edges, normalisation, temporal filtering, a region and the threshold-free cluster enhancement. Every output of the replica is FSL's bit for bit, in both modes.

The clean-room original gives FSL's values in fourteen of the fifteen calls, at 14 threads and at one and in both modes. The fifteenth is the mean filter with the 15 × 15 × 13 spherical kernel (1,313 voxels), which FSL convolves by its FFT and the original by a direct sum, adding each product to a single-precision sum as FSL does on its direct path: the two differ by up to 1.4 × 10<sup>−5</sup> of FSL's largest value, beyond §21's bound of 10<sup>−5</sup> (*open difference*). Against the exact result, computed here in float64 from the specification's definition, FSL's FFT lies within 1.5 × 10<sup>−7</sup> of the largest value and the original within 1.4 × 10<sup>−5</sup>: over 1,313 terms its single-precision sum is the less accurate of the two, while FSL's FFT error stays near single precision's of the image's largest value ([findings 31](repo:docs/findings/fsl-maths-original.md)). The record's synthetic FFT cases, on small images, stay within 2.6 × 10<sup>−6</sup>. A follow-up of the original: the products added in double precision where FSL would take its FFT (the exact result rounded once lies 2 × 10<sup>−7</sup> from FSL's output on this call), FSL's direct path keeping its single-precision sum bit for bit. ## GLIBC-07: glibc's double-precision functions {#glibc}

FSL's build takes its mathematical functions from glibc 2.35, whose double-precision `sin`, `cos`, `tan`, `asin`, `acos`, `atan`, `exp`, `log` and `pow` are not always correctly rounded: for a small share of arguments the result is one unit in the last place from the exact value. LARMORX computes them correctly rounded, the same on every platform (CORE-MATH). In fslmaths' default type, float, the double result is rounded to float and no value differs; with `-dt double` the last place shows. The table runs each function on a real image (FSL's mean of ds005040's series, scaled into the function's range): for `exp` and `log` the reproduce mode gives glibc's own bits and the default mode corrects them with a notice; for the six functions whose glibc code is IBM's Accurate Mathematical Library (LGPL), no permissive code gives glibc's bits, so the replica is correctly rounded in both modes and differs from FSL on those values (*expected divergence*). glibc chooses the build of each of these functions at run time (an FMA build on every x86-64 CPU since 2013, as on the oracle machine); FSL's own double results therefore depend on the CPU, and "glibc's bits" here are the FMA build's.

{{ ev.table('fsl-maths', 'glibc-double') }}

The clean-room original computes all of them correctly rounded, in both modes: its values are the replica's default's, one unit in the last place from FSL's where glibc's are not correctly rounded, within its record's threshold of one unit per function. Its reproduce mode does not yet give glibc's bits for `exp`, `log` and `pow`, which the specification asks of it (glibc 2.35 takes them from Arm's Optimized Routines, under the MIT licence): a follow-up.

{{ ev.table('fsl-maths', 'glibc-double-original') }}

The record's two expected divergences are both GLIBC-07. [`fn/double-tan`](#case=fn/double-tan): `-dt double -tan`, 3 of 210 values one unit in the last place. [`difftest/fft-twiddles-glibc-sincos`](#case=difftest/fft-twiddles-glibc-sincos), from random testing: a mean filter of a 30 × 1 × 3 float image that fslmaths convolves by the FFT, 1 of 90 voxels one unit in the last place. fslmaths' FFT (Armadillo's Kiss FFT) takes each twiddle factor from glibc's `sincos`, which is not correctly rounded on 0.148 % of the angles (of the transform lengths 1 to 256, 80 hold a factor one unit in the last place from the exact one), while the replica computes the factors correctly rounded. Given FSL's own factors, the replica's FFT gives Armadillo's values on every length from 1 to 256 (0 of 197,376 values differ) and this case FSL's output bit for bit ([the probe](repo:validation/probes/fsl-avwutils-lib/README.md)): the difference is the factors, nothing else. It is rare: a float result usually rounds it away (the FFT convolution of the real series above is FSL's bit for bit), and fMRIPrep's calls convolve by separable sums. An exact reproduction of glibc's functions is planned by a clean-room implementation of their behaviour.

## Random testing {#random}

Differential random testing's trial of this part: 24 minutes of random chains of operations on random images, each run by FSL 6.0.7.23 (through the oracle cache) and by the replica, every file compared.

The clean-room original's trial drew the replica trial's chains again and more (so the replica trial's runs of FSL were served by the oracle cache): 150,000 random chains of one to four operations in 20 minutes, each compared as the record compares. 143,191 agreed within the thresholds and 804 differed only by a correction the default mode names. ## Speed {#speed}

fslmaths runs on one thread; the clean-room original runs on as many as it is given, with the same result. On fMRIPrep's goodvoxels chain (its fourteen fslmaths calls and four fslstats calls in order) it takes 0.38 s against FSL's 1.70 s, with half FSL's memory. On the heavy operations of a real BOLD series and T1w it is 1.8 to 42 times faster than FSL on one thread and up to 356 times on 14: the largest factors on FSL's FFT path, whose padded transforms cost FSL 28.7 s and 228 s (and 4 GiB) where the original's direct sum takes 3.0 s and 5.4 s on one thread. The replica, a translation, is faster than FSL on most calls and slower on `-bptf` ([the report](repo:docs/benchmarks/fsl-maths.md): informal, one run each, on a machine shared with another agent's work). `combine_masks` takes milliseconds in every implementation, below the report's resolution, so it is left out of the chart; the table holds it.

## Corrections by default {#corrections}

The replica corrects FSL's deterministic defects by default and warns, with `upstream_defects="reproduce"` (`LARMORX_UPSTREAM_DEFECTS=reproduce`) giving FSL's behaviour byte for byte where FSL's is defined ([register](repo:docs/findings/upstream-defects.md)); where FSL crashes or reads memory it never wrote, the replica refuses in both modes or gives a defined result. Values converted to an integer type outside its range, or NaN, are clamped (NaN to 0) instead of wrapping around (FSL-151). The thresholds, `-bin`, `-binv` and `-mas` bound their range without wrapping or rounding back to the maximum (FSL-152). A NaN first voxel no longer makes the image's minimum and maximum NaN, and NaN voxels stay out of the robust range and of the reductions' maxima and minima (FSL-153). `-dt char` and `-dt input` on a uint8 image compute in unsigned bytes (FSL-154). `-dt input` on int8, uint16, uint32, int64, uint64 or float128 images, where FSL writes nothing and exits with status 0, computes in the closest type and writes the result (FSL-155). Integer divisions by zero are refused instead of crashing (FSL-156). `-odt input` on int8 and uint16 images rounds as the other integer outputs do and writes int8 as int8 (FSL-157). An operation missing its arguments is refused instead of reading past the command line (FSL-158), and an image of another size than the input for `-div`, `-rem`, `-max` and `-min` is refused (FSL-159). A Gaussian kernel of zero width leaves the image as it is instead of making it NaN (FSL-160); a kernel without voxels, an unknown kernel type and a negative width are refused (FSL-161). `-fmedian`, `-dilD`, `-rank` and `-ranknorm` leave NaN values out instead of stopping (FSL-162). `-subsamp2` and `-subsamp2offc` of a 4D image subsample every volume (FSL-163); `-dog_edge` of a 4D image filters every volume (FSL-164); `-fillh` of a single slice fills it (FSL-165); `-tensor_decomp` leaves a voxel whose tensor holds NaN or infinity at 0 (FSL-166). The standard deviation along a dimension takes the clamped variance, 0 for a single value (FSL-167); the percentiles leave NaN out (FSL-168), and so do `-pval`, `-pval0` and `-cpval` (FSL-169). `-ztop` of an infinite z gives 0 or 1 (FSL-170); `-bptf` with a width beyond an int is refused (FSL-171); `-ptoz` of a p-value below about 1.1 × 10<sup>−16</sup> gives its z (FSL-172). `-tfce` and `-tfceS` refuse a step that cannot advance (FSL-174), connectivities other than 6, 18 and 26 (FSL-176) and a voxel outside the image (FSL-178); `-tfce` of an image without positive values gives zeros (FSL-175); `-tfceS` sizes its arrays for every threshold (FSL-177). `-roc` refuses a noise image of another size (FSL-179) and a truth image without true or false voxels (FSL-180). A value more than about two billion bin widths above a histogram's range counts in its last bin (FSL-187: the robust range of `-thrp`, `-thrP`, `-uthrp`, `-uthrP`, shared with fslstats, BET and FLIRT). `-subsamp2` of an image 3 voxels wide computes its unused background value without writing past it (FSL-75, FLIRT's). glibc's double `exp`, `log` and `pow` are correctly rounded (GLIBC-07, [above](#glibc)).

The clean-room original makes the same corrections by default with the same notices, and its reproduce mode follows FSL where FSL's behaviour is defined and known from the specification and black-box runs (FSL-75: no output voxel depends on the corrupted value, so the original names it in both modes and writes its output). It adds three that its black-box runs found, registered with the same switches: with a kernel large enough for FSL's FFT, one NaN or infinite voxel makes FSL's whole volume NaN, where its direct sum for smaller kernels leaves NaN only within the kernel's reach; the default keeps that local result (FSL-191). On the same path FSL applies the kernel mirrored along every axis, where its direct sum applies it as stored, so the result of a kernel that is not point-symmetric depends on the kernel's and the image's sizes; the default keeps the direct sum's orientation at every size (FSL-192). `-roc` with a curve file it cannot write (a folder that does not exist) writes no curve and says nothing; the default stops with an error naming the file (FSL-193). Its double-precision functions are correctly rounded in both modes ([above](#glibc)).

## Known limits {#limits}

Both implementations are experimental. The clean-room original is held to thresholds, not to FSL's bytes: where FSL convolves a large kernel by its FFT its single-precision direct sum can lie beyond §21's bound (the mean filter above: 1.4 × 10<sup>−5</sup>; a double-precision sum there is its follow-up); FSL's arithmetic of `-subsamp2` under the floating types, the last bits of Φ and Φ<sup>−1</sup> in double precision and the eigenvectors' signs are questions for its specification; its reproduce mode does not give glibc's `exp`, `log` and `pow` bits yet; `erf` and `erfc` (in `-ztop`) come from the `libm` crate until LARMORX's own correctly rounded ones exist; and random testing left 10 differences open (above). Its record's standard tier holds fMRIPrep's calls on three oracle runs like the replica's. The replica's record's standard tier holds fMRIPrep's calls on three oracle runs; its full tier, the data panel and long random testing run in the final validation phase. The goodvoxels calls use a stand-in for the cortical ribbon, the segmentation's grey matter: they are to be checked again with a real ribbon once an fMRIPrep run with FreeSurfer's reconstruction exists. glibc's double-precision `sin`, `cos`, `tan`, `asin`, `acos` and `atan`, and the FFT's twiddle factors (glibc's `sincos`), are correctly rounded in both modes: their glibc bits wait for a clean-room implementation of glibc's behaviour. Images compressed with zstd or bzip2 are decompressed whole before they are read (gzip and uncompressed images are read as a stream). The other five platforms are held to LARMORX's own Linux output by the [golden tests](repo:docs/validation/golden.md) (the clean-room original: the command line in three cases and the typed wrappers in four, each family with a path that makes NaN from numbers, so x86-64's NaN bits are checked on ARM too). The benchmark is informal: one run each, on a shared machine.
