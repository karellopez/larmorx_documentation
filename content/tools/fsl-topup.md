## What it is

An echo-planar image is displaced along its phase-encoding axis wherever the magnetic field departs from its nominal value: by the off-resonance (in Hz) times the read-out time, in voxels, and its intensity is compressed or stretched by the displacement's Jacobian. Two images with opposite phase encodings are displaced in opposite directions, so the field that makes them agree can be estimated. topup models the field as a cubic (or quadratic) B-spline, resamples each image through the displacement the field gives it (and, with `--estmov`, through its own rigid-body movement), multiplies by the Jacobian, and minimises the squared differences between the corrected images plus a regularisation of the field's bending energy (or membrane energy) weighted by `--lambda`, level by level: each level with its knot spacing (`--warpres`), subsampling, smoothing and number of iterations, by Levenberg-Marquardt or scaled conjugate gradients. It writes the field's coefficients and the movements (`--out`), and on request the corrected images (`--iout`), the field in Hz (`--fout`), each image's displacement field (`--dfout`), rigid-body matrix (`--rbmout`) and Jacobian (`--jacout`), and a log. applytopup corrects other images with topup's result: by Jacobian modulation of each image alone (`--method=jac`), by the least-squares restoration of pairs with opposite phase encodings into one image (`lsr`, the default), or by one of three undocumented variational Bayes restorations (`vb2D`, `vb3D`, `vb4D`).

fMRIPrep calls topup for every reversed-phase-encoding field map, in sdcflows' PEPOLAR workflow (`fmap_preproc_wf/<estimator>/topup`), on the average of each phase encoding's images merged into one series, with sdcflows' configuration `b02b0.cnf` (seven levels from 14 mm to 4 mm knots, without movements): `topup --config=b02b0.cnf --datain=<encoding file> --imain=<merged pair> --out --iout --fout --jacout=jac --logout --rbmout=xfm --dfout=warpfield`. sdcflows turns the coefficients into the field map fMRIPrep uses; fMRIPrep does not call applytopup, its own resampler applies the field.

```bash
larmorx fsl topup --imain=b0_pair --datain=acqparams.txt --config=b02b0.cnf --out=tu --fout=field --iout=corrected
larmorx fsl applytopup --imain=dwi_up,dwi_dn --inindex=1,2 --datain=acqparams.txt --topup=tu --out=hifi
```

```python
import larmorx as lx

acq = [[0, 1, 0, 0.0138], [0, -1, 0, 0.0138]]   # phase-encoding vector and read-out time (s)
r = lx.fsl.topup("b0_pair.nii.gz", acq, config="b02b0.cnf", fsldir="/opt/fsl", corrected=True)
field_hz = r.field                     # lx.Image on the input's grid
hifi = lx.fsl.applytopup(["dwi_up.nii.gz", "dwi_dn.nii.gz"], acq, [1, 2], r).corrected
```

FSL is distributed under the FSL Licence, which allows non-commercial use only, so LARMORX keeps its FSL tools in two implementations: the exact replica translated from FSL's source, in the separate package `larmorx-nc` (`pip install larmorx-nc`), and a clean-room original in LARMORX itself, written from a behavioural specification without FSL's code. `implementation="auto"` (and `larmorx fsl topup`) runs the replica when `larmorx-nc` is installed and the clean-room original otherwise; `implementation="original"` (on the command line `LARMORX_IMPLEMENTATION=original`) chooses the original. The clean-room original is written from topup's behavioural specification ([specs/topup.md](repo:specs/topup.md)), the papers (Andersson, Skare and Ashburner 2003; Smith et al. 2004), FSL's user guides and black-box runs of FSL's programs, never from FSL's code: every option, message, exit status and failure case of both programs (the configuration files, both minimisers per level, both regularisers, both spline orders, the precisions and interpolations, the movements, every output, `--debug`, `--trace` and `--profile`; applytopup's `jac`, `lsr`, `vb2D`, `vb3D` and `vb4D`), with `sin`, `cos` and `exp` correctly rounded on every platform. It is built for speed: the B-spline field's products run along whole lines and planes, the Levenberg-Marquardt steps by preconditioned conjugate gradients on the Hessian stored by its diagonals, the arrays recycled, and every parallel loop in fixed blocks summed in a fixed order, so its result is the same bits at any thread count (below, *Speed*). Where the specification leaves a convention open it follows FSL's as black-box runs measured it: an oblique phase encoding wraps along no axis, the scaled conjugate gradients' second gradient keeps the current point's regulariser weight, `--debug`'s images are written in FSL's order. The replica follows FSL 6.0.7.23, runs none of FSL's programs and needs no FSL installation (FSL's configurations by name need its `etc/flirtsch` folder, or `fsldir`); the FSL libraries topup needs (the B-spline fields with their bending- and membrane-energy regularisers, the sparse matrices, the non-linear optimisers, the warps, the spline interpolation with its derivatives, LAPACK's and OpenBLAS's arithmetic as FSL's build calls it) are translated once into `larmorx-nc`'s shared core, beside those of mcflirt, BET, FLIRT and FAST. How to read what follows: [the validation guide](/validation/#reading).

**The C library's last digits (since 2026-11-04).** topup smooths the images at each level of its schedule (`--fwhm`) with a Gaussian whose weights FSL computes with glibc's `exp`, the C library of Linux, whose results are now and then one unit in the last place from the exact value rounded once. LARMORX computes `exp` correctly rounded, the same on every platform; since 2026-11-04 the clean-room original's `reproduce` mode computes it exactly as glibc 2.35 does, and its default correctly rounded (GLIBC-07 in the [register](repo:docs/findings/upstream-defects.md), named nowhere: whether a fit moved with such last bits cannot be told without running it twice, the user's decision of 2026-11-04). On topup this changed no value: the weights are kept in single precision, and on every case of the record no number moved, and the two modes still agree on fMRIPrep's calls.

**The clean-room catch-up (2026-11-05).** The specification's answers of 2026-11-03 ([specs/topup.md](repo:specs/topup.md) §13) are now in the clean-room original, every example below run again with it: its reproduce mode takes FSL's forward differences of single-precision rotation matrices for the movements' derivatives (FSL-201: the first gradients of FSL's `--debug` files to 2 × 10<sup>−5</sup> and 10<sup>−6</sup>; the default keeps the exact derivatives and names FSL-201 on every run that estimates the movements), lays FSL-145's coefficient count exactly and stops with FSL's error where a level refits a single coefficient, follows FSL-133's index on lines whose length does not divide 2<sup>32</sup> and lays FSL-134's field of another shape as FSL does; and in both modes the regulariser samples the quadratic B-splines' bending energy as FSL does, the spline prefilter starts from FSL's truncated sums on short axes, and applytopup's least-squares restoration builds its matrices row by row as FSL walks them. The record's cases that FSL-133, FSL-134 and FSL-145 kept apart now pass. Random testing after the catch-up found one more defect of FSL, registered and corrected by default (FSL-242: a level of scaled conjugate gradients whose last step is rejected ends at that worse point), and that FSL counts every such iteration toward `--miter`, which the original now does too ([below](#random)).

## Membrane energy, and a correction by default {#membrane}

With `--regmod=membrane_energy` and Levenberg-Marquardt (the record's case `opt/membrane-lm`, on the half-resolution pair), FSL takes the regulariser's Hessian in other units than its cost and gradient: each first derivative divided by the voxel size in millimetres, where the cost counts in voxels. The replica's default takes the Hessian in the cost's units (FSL-117), so its steps follow the cost they minimise; its reproduce mode gives FSL's bytes. ## Movements estimated, and a correction by default {#movements}

With `--estmov=1` topup also estimates each volume's rigid-body movement against the first (the record's case `opt/levels`: two levels on the half-resolution pair, the first at subsampling 2 by Levenberg-Marquardt with the movements, the second at full resolution by scaled conjugate gradients; every output written). fMRIPrep's configuration estimates none; FSL's own `b02b0.cnf` does, in its first five levels. FSL takes the cost's derivatives with respect to the rotations as forward differences of rotation matrices built in single precision, so its gradient and its Hessian of the movements are not the cost's, and its minimisers end elsewhere than the cost's minimum: FSL-201, registered on 2026-11-03 with the specification's answers to the clean-room original's questions, after measuring that the exact derivatives end at a lower or equal cost in 18 of 22 runs (the other four, cut short, 0.004 to 0.13 % higher) and lower in all 8 random cases whose volumes moved. The replica's default computes the exact derivatives (*deliberate correction*); its reproduce mode gives FSL's bytes. The clean-room original's default computes them too, and since 2026-11-05 its reproduce mode takes FSL's forward differences: the movement table below the viewer shows the two defaults within 6 × 10<sup>−6</sup> mm and 3 × 10<sup>−7</sup> rad of each other, where FSL's movements lie 3 × 10<sup>−4</sup> mm and 8 × 10<sup>−5</sup> rad from them; the original's reproduce mode ends 5 × 10<sup>−4</sup> mm and 1.3 × 10<sup>−4</sup> rad from FSL. Its first gradients are FSL's, but FSL's path here is not determined beyond its inputs' last bits (a rotation's derivative jumps by about 2 % when its angle's rounding to single precision flips), which is why the record holds this case as an expected divergence, measured against FSL's own spread.

## applytopup: least-squares restoration {#lsr}

applytopup's default method (the record's case `at/lsr-y`): the two images of opposite phase encodings restored into one, line by line along the phase encoding, with the field and movements of FSL's topup on the same pair. FSL resamples the first image with trilinear interpolation whatever `--interp` asks (FSL-132); the replica's default uses the method asked for, spline by default, and its reproduce mode gives FSL's bytes.

## applytopup: Jacobian modulation {#jac}

`--method=jac` (the record's case `at/jac-y`): one image corrected by itself, resampled along the phase encoding and multiplied by the displacement's Jacobian. FSL-132 reaches it too. With a phase encoding along z, which topup does not estimate, FSL leaves the image uncorrected without a word; the clean-room original's default refuses such a call (FSL-144), its reproduce mode gives FSL's output.

## applytopup --method=vb3D, and a correction by default {#vb3d}

One of applytopup's undocumented variational Bayes restorations (the record's case `at/vb3d-y`): a smoothness prior within and between slices whose weights are estimated over 20 iterations. FSL builds the prior's coupling between slices with half its weight, so the precision it assembles is not positive definite and its iterations diverge: FSL's output spans about ±4 × 10<sup>10</sup> for an input of at most 1.1 × 10<sup>5</sup>, and the replica's reproduce mode gives the same bytes. The replica's default gives the prior its full weight (FSL-143), and its restoration stays in the input's range, as vb2D's does. FSL's panel is shown in the default's window; choose *each image's own* window to read it.

## Speed {#speed}

topup at one thread is how fMRIPrep runs it; with `--nthr` FSL runs threaded, and its result then changes (FSL-116). On fMRIPrep's two calls the clean-room original takes 2.8 and 7.7 s on one thread, 10 to 12 times faster than FSL at one thread, and 0.8 and 2.0 s on 14 threads, 39 to 41 times, the same bits at both; FSL at `--nthr=14` takes 12.3 and 27.4 s. applytopup's least-squares restoration of ds005040's pair takes it 0.13 s on one thread and 0.04 s on 14, against FSL's 0.42 s. The replica, a translation, runs at about FSL's speed in topup (29.7 and 71.4 s) and takes 3.5 s for the restoration ([the report](repo:docs/benchmarks/fsl-topup.md): informal, one run each, on a machine shared with another agent's work). The original's peak memory on fMRIPrep's call on ds005040, as the memory test measures it at one thread, is 74 MiB against FSL's 88.

## Corrections by default {#corrections}

The replica corrects FSL's deterministic defects by default and warns, with `upstream_defects="reproduce"` (`LARMORX_UPSTREAM_DEFECTS=reproduce`) giving FSL's behaviour byte for byte where FSL's is defined ([register](repo:docs/findings/upstream-defects.md)). In topup: above one thread (`--nthr`, or `FSL_NUM_THREADS` for some of its matrices), FSL's result changes with the thread count; the replica gives FSL's one-thread result at any count (FSL-116, never reproduced). The membrane energy's Hessian in the cost's units (FSL-117, above). With the movements estimated, the exact derivatives with respect to the rotations, where FSL takes forward differences of single-precision rotation matrices (FSL-201, since 2026-11-03, above). With `--regrid=1` and subsamplings whose largest does not divide the others, FSL stops at the first level with the smaller one; the replica's grid grows by their least common multiple (FSL-118). The field maps `--fout` and `--featout` write keep the input's intent parameters in place (FSL-119: FSL shifts them by one). A row-count mismatch of `--acqp` names the `--acqp` file (FSL-120). With `FSLDIR` unset and a configuration name not found, a clear error instead of FSL's exception (FSL-121). A configuration line longer than 1023 characters after its first word no longer ends the reading silently (FSL-122). A negative `--miter`, `--fwhm` or `--lambda`, which FSL uses as given (a negative `--fwhm` smooths nothing), is refused (FSL-123, FSL-125, FSL-126); a negative `--warpres` is refused in both modes (FSL-124: FSL's knot spacing wraps and it exhausts the memory). In applytopup: every volume resampled with the interpolation asked for (FSL-132, above); the least-squares restoration wraps a negative index around its line (FSL-133: FSL wraps it by 2^32 modulo the line's length, wrong for lengths that do not divide 2^32, such as 30 or 96); `--method=jac` with a field image scales the displacement along y by the y voxel size (FSL-134: FSL takes the x one), and with a field whose voxel sizes differ from the images' it modulates by the Jacobian of the displacement applied (FSL-273, 2026-11-06: FSL divides the field's derivatives by the field's own voxel sizes), and a field on another grid is refused (in both modes where FSL reads past its lines); an unknown `--method`, which FSL ignores silently (exit status 0, no output), and `lsr` or `vb*` without movements, where FSL aborts, are refused with a reason (FSL-135); the variational methods' counts of values in 64-bit integers (FSL-141: FSL's 32-bit ones wrap on very large images); vb3D and vb4D count the data points of every image (FSL-142) and build their prior with its full weight (FSL-143, above). Five defects of FSL's libraries that topup and applytopup never reach are in the register too (FSL-127 to FSL-131). The clean-room original makes the same corrections by default with the same notices, and its reproduce mode follows FSL where FSL's behaviour is defined; since 2026-11-05 that includes FSL-201's forward differences, FSL-133's index on lines whose length does not divide 2<sup>32</sup> and FSL-134's field in another shape, which the specification describes since its answers of 2026-11-03 (its default restores those lines circularly and refuses that field). It adds four that black-box runs found: `--method=jac` with a phase encoding along z leaves the image uncorrected silently, which the default refuses (FSL-144); `--splineorder=2` lays two coefficients on an axis shorter than two knot spacings, so the field is forced towards 0 at its far end, where the default lays the general count (FSL-145); and with volumes encoded along different axes FSL's gradient leaves out of each axis' term the volumes not encoded along it, where the default minimises with the cost's own gradient (FSL-146: the default's final cost was as low as FSL's or lower in 86 to 89 % of the trial's differing four-volume cases); and with scaled conjugate gradients a level whose last step is rejected ends at that worse point, where the default ends at the lowest cost the level found (FSL-242, 2026-11-05). The replica has the first three switches since 2026-11-03 (its reproduce mode lays FSL's count exactly, one coefficient on an axis shorter than one spacing, and gives FSL's error when a level refits a single coefficient).

## Known limits {#limits}

Both implementations are experimental. Each record's standard tier holds fMRIPrep's calls on its two oracle runs; the full tiers, the data panel's other PEPOLAR runs, long random testing and FSL's own band on every case run in the final validation phase. The clean-room original is held to thresholds, not to FSL's bytes: where the movements are estimated and do not converge it ends elsewhere than FSL even in its reproduce mode, which takes FSL's forward differences (FSL-201) along a path FSL's own last bits move, and on fits the data hardly determine (no regularisation, a few hundred voxels at a subsampled level) it can reach another minimum as low as FSL's. The questions it left for the specification until 2026-11-03 ([specs/topup.md](repo:specs/topup.md) §13) are followed since 2026-11-05; the catch-up's trial left new ones for it: the quadratic splines' bending energy on an axis of FSL-145's two coefficients (§4.6), the smoothing on an axis shorter than its kernel (§4.4), and a field image's Jacobian scales when its voxel sizes differ from the images' (§7.3). The replica: applytopup's restorations of lines of fewer than 8 or more than 256 voxels along the phase encoding, and band systems that need LAPACK's blocked band factorisation, are refused in both modes with a message: FSL's arithmetic there follows other paths of OpenBLAS and Armadillo, which the replica does not reproduce yet. FSL's results above one thread are not reproduced by either (they depend on the thread count: FSL-116). The other five platforms are held to LARMORX's own Linux output by the [golden tests](repo:docs/validation/golden.md) (the clean-room original: two levels with every output, the other options and FSL-117 and FSL-145 reproduced, applytopup's `lsr`, `jac` and `vb` methods, FSL-132 reproduced, the Python functions). The benchmark is informal: one run each, on a shared machine.
