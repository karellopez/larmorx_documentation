## What it is

BET finds the brain in a head image: a tessellated sphere (2562 vertices) is placed inside the head at its centre of gravity and deformed, a thousand times over, by three forces at each vertex: one along the surface that keeps the vertices evenly spread, one along the normal that keeps the surface smooth, and one along the normal that follows the intensities found inward, against a threshold between the image's robust minimum and the local maximum (`-f`). The brain is what the surface encloses. `bet` is the script around it, with variations: `-R` repeats the extraction from the brain's own centre, `-F` applies the first volume's mask to a 4D series, `-Z` pads an image that is short in z, `-S` and `-B` are SIENA's cleanups (the eyes and optic nerves; the bias field and the neck, through standard_space_roi, FLIRT, FAST, fslmaths and fslstats), and `-A` and `-A2` add betsurf's surfaces after a registration to MNI152. `betsurf` then casts intensity profiles along the brain surface's normals through the T1 (and a T2) to find the inner skull, the outer skull and the scalp, and deforms three more surfaces onto them.

fMRIPrep calls it on every BOLD run's reference, `bet <boldref> <boldref>_brain.nii.gz -f 0.20 -m`, niworkflows' first skull strip (`enhance_and_skullstrip_bold_wf`), and, where field maps correct the distortion, once more on the corrected reference (`skullstrip_bold_wf`).

```bash
larmorx fsl bet boldref.nii.gz boldref_brain.nii.gz -f 0.20 -m
larmorx fsl betsurf --t1only -o -m -s T1w.nii.gz T1w_brain_mesh.vtk T1w_to_MNI152.mat T1w
```

```python
import larmorx as lx

r = lx.fsl.bet("boldref.nii.gz", frac=0.2, mask=True)     # implementation="auto"
r.brain, r.mask
s = lx.fsl.betsurf("T1w.nii.gz", lx.fsl.bet("T1w.nii.gz", mesh=True).mesh, "T1w_to_MNI152.mat")
```

FSL is distributed under the FSL Licence, which allows non-commercial use only, so LARMORX keeps its FSL tools in two implementations: the exact replica translated from FSL's source, in the separate package `larmorx-nc` (`pip install larmorx-nc`), and a clean-room original in LARMORX itself, written from a behavioural specification ([specs/bet.md](repo:specs/bet.md)), the BET paper and runs of FSL's programs as black boxes, without FSL's code. `implementation="auto" | "replica" | "original"` chooses (on the command line `LARMORX_IMPLEMENTATION`); `auto` takes the replica when `larmorx-nc` is installed, else the original. The replica follows FSL 6.0.7.23 since the version update of 2026-10-29 (it was translated from 6.0.7.17's source; what changed between the two is in its findings), runs none of FSL's programs and needs no FSL installation; its FSL libraries are those every FSL replica of `larmorx-nc` shares (newimage, NewNifti, meshclass, the C library's maths as FSL's Linux build calls it). The original follows FSL 6.0.7.23 as well, is built for speed (every parallel step in the call's own pool of threads, with the same result at any thread count) and its memory peak is FSL's or lower on every measured call but `bet -R` (117 MiB, against FSL's 103 and the interpreter's 13). Its smoothness weight's `tanh` is glibc's by default, as FSL's, and the correctly rounded one on request (`--tanh correctly-rounded`, the user's decision of 2026-10-31). Since 2026-11-04 the replica runs `bet -S`, `-B`, `-A` and `-A2`: FSL's script for each, every program it runs the replica's own, called in the same process ([below](#options)), with FSL's standard_space_roi and fslroi as programs of their own ([below](#ssroi)) and the three standard images they read shipped inside `larmorx-nc` ([below](#data)). Since 2026-11-05 the clean-room original runs them as well, written from the specification ([specs/bet.md](repo:specs/bet.md) §5.3, [specs/standard_space_roi.md](repo:specs/standard_space_roi.md)): FSL's script for each, every program it runs (bet2, FLIRT, FAST, fslmaths, fslstats, `dc`'s arithmetic, standard_space_roi, fslroi, betsurf) its own clean-room program, in the same process, the standard images found by its data search ([below](#data)). How to read what follows: [the validation guide](/validation/#reading).

**The C library's last digits (since 2026-11-04).** bet2's radius and its surface's deformation take powers (`powf`) and betsurf's smoothing `exp` from glibc, the C library of Linux, whose results are now and then one unit in the last place from the exact value rounded once. LARMORX computes them correctly rounded, the same on every platform; since 2026-11-04 the clean-room original's `reproduce` mode computes them exactly as glibc 2.35 does, and its default correctly rounded (GLIBC-07 in the [register](repo:docs/findings/upstream-defects.md), named nowhere: whether a fitted surface moved with such last bits cannot be told without running it twice, the user's decision of 2026-11-04). No number of its record moved, and the original's runs of the examples below give the same bytes as before.

## The verdicts at a glance {#verdicts}

A clean-room original is written without FSL's source, so its record holds it to thresholds (specs/bet.md §11): exit statuses and messages FSL's, and every file FSL writes either bit-identical or, for the masks, within a Dice coefficient and a boundary distance; betsurf, whose three surfaces are deformed from thousands of profiles, is held to its masks (Dice ≥ 0.99, the boundary distance's 95th percentile ≤ 1 voxel), its meshes reported. A case is *bit-identical* when every value of every file is FSL's; *open* when a file only meets its threshold, or misses it.

## The variations that run FSL's other programs: `-S`, `-B`, `-A`, `-A2` {#options}

Four of the script's variations run other FSL programs. `-S` and `-B` are SIENA's cleanups: `-S` removes the eyes and optic nerves (bet2 for the raw brain and skull, standard_space_roi to cut the head to MNI152's dilated brain mask and bet2 again on it, FLIRT to bring MNI152's eye mask into the head, fslmaths' morphology, fslstats' mean and standard deviation of the brain and `dc` for a threshold 3.5 deviations above it); `-B` removes the bias field and the neck (standard_space_roi, FAST's bias field, fslmaths to divide it out, standard_space_roi again and the script itself with `-R`; with `-s`, standard_space_roi `-maskFOV` and bet2 for the skull). `-A` and `-A2` register the head to MNI152 with FLIRT and give bet2's mesh to betsurf (`-A2` with the T2w, which FLIRT first registers to the T1w). The replica runs FSL 6.0.7.23's script for each: every program it runs is the replica's own, called in the same process with the words bash would give it, with the script's temporary files, `-d` keeping them, messages and exit statuses; GNU `dc`'s arithmetic for `-S`'s threshold is replicated too. Its record compares them on synthetic heads (every option they pass on, `-d`, `-v`, the order of the variations: the last one given wins) and on six real heads of the data panel, shown below: each passes, every file FSL writes byte for byte.

Each example shows FSL 6.0.7.23's run (through the oracle cache: the record's own run), the replica in its default and reproduce modes and, for `-S` and `-B`, FSL's `bet` without the option on the same head (`-m`, with `-s` where the option writes a skull image): the comparison *what the option changes* is FSL against FSL, the option's effect, without a verdict. ## standard_space_roi and fslroi {#ssroi}

`standard_space_roi` is FSL's script that cuts a head with a region of the standard space: FLIRT registers the head to MNI152 (or another reference, `-ssref`), `convert_xfm` inverts the matrix, and a mask (`-maskFOV`, `-maskMASK`, `-maskNONE`) and a region (`-roiFOV`, `-roiMASK`, `-roiNONE`) are brought back into the head with FLIRT's `-applyxfm`, then applied with fslmaths and fslroi (`-b` is MNI152's dilated brain mask, thresholded, the call `bet -S` and `-B` make). `fslroi` takes a box or a range of volumes out of an image (`bet -F` and `-Z` use it). Both are replicated since 2026-11-04, from FSL 6.0.7.23 (fsl-flirt 2111.4, fsl-avwutils 2209.6), as programs of `larmorx-nc` (`larmorx fsl standard_space_roi`, `larmorx fsl fslroi`; `lx.fsl.standard_space_roi`, `lx.fsl.fslroi`, [their API page](repo:docs/api/fsl-roi.md)); fslpy's `imcp` and `immv`, which the scripts call, are replicated with them. Since 2026-11-05 both are clean-room originals too (`implementation="original"`, Apache-2.0, written from [specs/standard_space_roi.md](repo:specs/standard_space_roi.md), built for speed), whose cases are in the original's record: fslroi's 26, 24 with FSL's files bit for bit and 2 rejected by both alike; standard_space_roi's 25, 17 within the specification's thresholds (its files FSL's where its FLIRT gives FSL's matrix, else the masks Dice ≥ 0.995, the output's box within one voxel and, given FSL's matrix, every file bit for bit; the six real heads bit for bit) and 8 rejected by both alike. The replica's record holds their cases beside bet's ([the record](repo:docs/validation/fsl-bet-replica.md)): of standard_space_roi's 25 (every option on synthetic heads, every output type, the failures, and six real T1w and T2w heads), 16 give FSL's files byte for byte, 8 are rejected by both alike, and 1 is the expected divergence above (fslpy's conversion with nibabel); of fslroi's 26 (every argument form, data type, header and format), 24 give FSL's files byte for byte and 2 are rejected by both. Four defects of the script and two of fslroi are corrected or refused by default, each named, with `reproduce` giving FSL's behaviour (below, [Corrections](#corrections)). The example: `-b` on the data panel's youngest head, whose registration to an adult template a small head makes harder.

## The standard images the variations read {#data}

`-S`, `-B` and standard_space_roi read FSL's MNI152 T1 at 2 mm and its dilated brain mask, `-S` its eye mask, `-A` and `-A2` the T1 again (FLIRT's reference): three files of FSL 6.0.7.23's `fsl-data_standard` 2208.0 package, 1,420,897 bytes in all ([findings](repo:docs/findings/fsl-bet.md)). FAST's `-b` reads no tissue priors, and betsurf no image of its own. `larmorx-nc` ships these three files byte for byte, with FSL's Licence (the package is part of FSL, under its non-commercial terms), the MNI ICBM 152 notice and a manifest of each file's origin, size and SHA-256, which a test checks; the wheel installs them beside the program, so the variations run without an FSL installation. The replica reads them from `LARMORX_FSL_DATA` when it is set, else from `$FSLDIR/data` as FSL's script does, else from its own copy, and refuses before running anything, naming the missing image, when none has it. The clean-room original never ships them (LARMORX itself is Apache-2.0): it reads them as data from a directory named on its call or by `LARMORX_FSL_DATA`, from `larmorx-nc`'s copy when that package is installed, from `$FSLDIR/data`, or else from a cache filled once by `larmorx fetch fsl-standard`, a download of FSL's `fsl-data_standard` 2208.0 package checked against its checksum, of which only the three images are kept (in `LARMORX_DATA_DIR`, else the platform's user cache directory).

## Corrections by default {#corrections}

Both implementations correct FSL's deterministic defects by default and warn, with `upstream_defects="reproduce"` (`LARMORX_UPSTREAM_DEFECTS=reproduce`) giving FSL's behaviour byte for byte ([register](repo:docs/findings/upstream-defects.md)). Among them: bet2 removes `.hdr` from a name wherever it stands (FSL-40); a mask's fill can start outside the image (FSL-43: on a single slice; FSL then reaches the image's voxels through its linear index, and memory beyond it); the `bet` script splits names with blanks (FSL-44), leaves `-Z`'s outputs padded when FSL is not on the `PATH` (FSL-45), removes files it did not create (FSL-46) and, with `-R`, loses a leading `-e` through bash's `echo` (FSL-63); one skull profile with an empty search range ends betsurf without a message or an output (FSL-50); `--t1only` exits 0 when it fails (FSL-54); an image with two or more extensions gets a wrong data offset (FSL-56); since FSL 6.0.7.23, a name holding `.img` or `.hdr` opens another file (FSL-60) and `--ct` with `--binaryMask` converts the mask instead of the image (FSL-62). Inputs that make no sense, which FSL uses silently, are refused by default with a message naming the entry: NaN or meaningless numeric options (FSL-42), a T2 on another grid (FSL-48), a short matrix file (FSL-53), NaN inside the brain (FSL-55), and since FSL 6.0.7.23 a `--binaryMask` on another grid than the input (FSL-61). Where FSL crashes on what it leaves undefined, the default refuses and `reproduce` fills it deterministically, with a warning: the median of an empty list (FSL-41: a constant or binary image; FSL 6.0.7.23 fixed it, taking the robust maximum), mesh values FSL never sets (FSL-52), a fill from a centre that is not a number (FSL-43). Two of FSL's choices are kept and documented (FSL-49, FSL-57). Since 2026-11-04, with the variations and the scripts they run: `-S`'s eye threshold is computed by `dc` from what fslstats prints, so a mean printed with an exponent or a minus sign becomes `dc` commands (FSL-216: the numbers read as numbers by default; a mean that is not a number refused); standard_space_roi splits names with blanks (FSL-211), removes every file named like its temporary files (FSL-212), applies a `maskthr` it finds in the environment (FSL-213) and checks no step's status, going on after a failed program on missing files (FSL-214: refused by default, stopping at the first failure); fslroi exits with status 0 after the errors it reports (FSL-215), and fslroi and fslmerge write an int8 image as uint8 with the same bytes, so `bet -F` and `-Z` run bet2 on the wrapped values (FSL-217: int8 kept). Inside the variations the corrections of the replicated programs apply as in each program (FLIRT's FSL-09 and FSL-14, fslstats' FSL-182, FAST's FSL-202), named where they change a value.

## Known limits {#limits}

The clean-room original is experimental: betsurf's surfaces differ from FSL's where few profiles vote and at the scalp (one real head's scalp misses its threshold), and a centre on a neurologically stored image can start bet2 a few bits elsewhere (the 3.5-year-old's `-B`); standard_space_roi's output header on random headers is one float unit from FSL's in its translation, or a form's translation whole voxels apart where the qform and sform differ, or in single precision where FSL writes NIfTI-2, and fslpy's conversion of an input in another file type is not yet checked byte for byte; `-B` and `-A2` on tiny heads where FSL's script fails midway print other messages. These wait for answers in its specification or for BET's next clean-room part ([findings](repo:docs/findings/fsl-bet-original.md)). The replica does not replicate fslpy's `imcp` conversion of an image to another file type with nibabel (standard_space_roi with `-maskNONE -roiNONE` on an input whose type is not `FSLOUTPUTTYPE`'s), which it refuses clearly in both modes; random testing's short trial of 2026-11-04 (2,699 random calls of bet2, bet with every variation, betsurf, fslroi and standard_space_roi; 2,580 agreed, 70 differed only by a notice) met it 8 times. Its other differences were two notices of the replica's own, fixed with permanent cases, the corrections inside the programs, which change only intermediate values there, and FLIRT's buffer overrun on constant and tiny images (FSL-75), after which FSL's FLIRT crashes or finishes as its memory's layout decides ([findings](repo:docs/findings/fsl-bet.md)). fMRIPrep's image has no `dc`, so its own `bet -R` and `-Z` stop there; the T1w example shows no run of it. FSL 6.0.7.23 computes the inverses, solves and determinants of its 4 × 4 matrices through LAPACK (Armadillo 14.2.1 on OpenBLAS 0.3.33), where 6.0.7.17 used closed forms: the last bits change. The replica follows that arithmetic since 2026-10-29, checked bit for bit against FSL's own library on 30.2 million seeded matrices of 22 kinds (random, affine, rigid, diagonal, triangular, singular, NaN and infinite entries among them). Random testing had found the change in bet2's `-c` on an image stored neurologically (a slightly different start, 18 of 15,360 voxels) and in `fslstats -C`'s sign of zero under `bet -R` on a one-voxel-wide image: two cases of the record now, both bit-identical. OpenBLAS chooses its kernels by the CPU, and on CPUs with AVX-512 they fuse multiplications and additions, so FSL's own last bits depend on the machine (FSL-64): the records run FSL on the kernels of CPUs without AVX-512, the order the replica follows on every platform. The other five platforms are held to LARMORX's own Linux output by the [golden tests](repo:docs/validation/golden.md).
